# Best Funnel Analytics Tools in 2026

Every funnel leaks, but not every business knows where. About **70%** of online shopping carts are abandoned, according to Baymard Institute, and the median landing page converts only around **6.6%** of visitors, according to Unbounce data. Without the right analytics tool, those drop-offs stay hidden inside a single "overall conversion rate."

Funnel analytics tools solve that by showing how many people move through each step, where they leave and, in some cases, why. The choice has also grown more interesting in 2026. Amplitude moved to event-based pricing in July 2026, Hotjar is being folded into Contentsquare, and tools like PostHog now offer **1 million free events a month**.

In this guide, I'll explain what funnel analytics tools do, how to choose one, and compare the best options in 2026: their features, pricing, pros, cons and who each one is best for.

## What Is a Funnel Analytics Tool?

**A funnel analytics tool tracks how people move through each step of a journey, such as landing page, sign-up, checkout and purchase, and shows where they drop off.**

Basic web analytics tells you how many people visited and how many bought. Funnel analytics goes further. It connects the steps in between, so you can see that, for example, 40% of visitors click "Start trial," but only 12% of those complete onboarding.

Funnel analytics tools generally fall into four groups:

- **Visual funnel mapping tools:** Show your funnel as a diagram with live data, like Funnelytics.
- **Web analytics platforms:** Track your whole website, with funnel reports built in, like Google Analytics 4.
- **Product analytics tools:** Track user behavior inside apps and SaaS products, like Mixpanel, Amplitude and PostHog.
- **Behavior analytics tools:** Show why people drop off with heatmaps and recordings, like Hotjar and Microsoft Clarity.

Many businesses combine one tool for the "where" (numbers) with another for the "why" (behavior).

## 1. Funnelytics

**Best for: Marketers, agencies and course creators who want visual funnel maps with live data.**

Funnelytics is built specifically around marketing funnels. You drag traffic sources, pages and actions onto a canvas, connect them with arrows and see real visitor and conversion numbers on each step. It's one of the few tools that lets you **plan** a funnel before building it and then **measure** it on the same map.

### Key Features

- **Drag-and-drop funnel canvas** for planning and mapping
- **Live data on each step** once the tracking script is installed
- **Traffic source and UTM tracking** on the map
- **Cross-domain tracking** through a simple domain whitelist
- **KPIs** for key actions like sign-ups and purchases
- **Exports** of maps as images and reports as PDFs

### Pricing

According to [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/200577/Funnelytics/pricing/), Funnelytics offers a free version, **Performance Lite at $200 a month** (one-code install, automatic page and traffic-source tracking, cross-domain tracking, unlimited seats) and **Performance Plus at $500 a month** (adding purchase and form tracking, custom events, segmentation and 1-on-1 training). It's rated **4.4 out of 5** from 31 reviews. The free trial requires a credit card.

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:** Very visual, easy to explain to clients, great for funnel planning, unlimited seats.

**Cons:** Expensive for small businesses, not a replacement for site-wide analytics, purchase tracking only on the higher plan.

For a full hands-on breakdown, see this detailed [Funnelytics review](https://bloggervoice.com/funnelytics-review/), which covers features, pricing, pros and cons.

## 2. Google Analytics 4

**Best for: Almost every website as a free starting point, especially businesses using Google Ads.**

Google Analytics 4 is the most widely used analytics tool on the web. It tracks events across your site, lets you mark important actions as key events and includes a **funnel exploration** report that shows step-by-step drop-off.

### Key Features

- **Event-based tracking** of page views, clicks, forms and purchases
- **Funnel explorations** with open and closed funnels and breakdowns by device or source
- **Path explorations** to see what people do before and after a step
- **Ecommerce tracking** from product view to purchase
- **Google Ads, Search Console and BigQuery integrations**

### Pricing

GA4 is **free**. Google Analytics 360 is a paid enterprise version with custom pricing. On free properties, event data for explorations is kept for 2 months by default, adjustable up to 14 months, and up to 30 key events are allowed.

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:** Free, complete site-wide analytics, deep Google Ads integration, huge community.

**Cons:** Steep learning curve, funnel reports are less visual, data retention limits for explorations.

## 3. Mixpanel

**Best for: Product and SaaS teams that want fast, flexible funnel and retention reports.**

Mixpanel is a product analytics tool focused on user actions. Its funnel reports are quick to build, and you can break down conversions by any property, like plan type, country or acquisition channel. It's especially strong for tracking in-app behavior, such as onboarding steps or feature adoption.

### Key Features

- **Funnels** with conversion windows and breakdowns
- **Flows** to see the paths users take
- **Retention reports** to see who comes back
- **Cohorts** for targeting specific user groups
- **Unlimited data history and seats**, even on the free plan

### Pricing

According to [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/158740/Mixpanel/pricing/), Mixpanel has a **Free** plan, a **Growth** plan starting at **$25 a month**, and custom **Enterprise** pricing. Growth adds unlimited saved reports and cohorts, data modeling and email support. It's rated **4.5 out of 5** from 146 reviews.

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:** Fast funnel reports, strong segmentation, generous free plan.

**Cons:** Needs careful event planning, less suited to marketing-site analytics, costs can rise with scale.

## 4. Amplitude

**Best for: Growing SaaS companies and apps that want deep product analytics with experiments.**

Amplitude is one of the most advanced product analytics platforms. Its funnel analysis shows conversion and drop-off, and it can highlight which behaviors are linked to users converting or sticking around.

### Key Features

- **Funnel analysis** with conversion drivers
- **Pathfinder** to explore user journeys
- **Retention and cohort analysis**
- **Experiments, guides and surveys**, depending on plan
- **AI agents**, added to all tiers in 2026

### Pricing

In July 2026, Amplitude moved from user-based (MTU) pricing to **event-based** pricing, according to PricingSaaS. The Starter plan was renamed **Free**, and the **Plus** plan now starts at $0 with 2 million events included and scales up to 70 million events. Growth and Enterprise plans require contacting sales.

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:** Very powerful analysis, strong for product-led growth, AI features on all plans.

**Cons:** Can be complex for beginners, advanced features on higher plans, recent pricing changes mean you should check limits carefully.

## 5. PostHog

**Best for: Developers and startups that want an all-in-one product toolkit with a large free tier.**

PostHog combines product analytics, funnels, session replay, feature flags, A/B testing and surveys in one platform. It's popular with technical teams because it's flexible and transparent about usage-based pricing.

### Key Features

- **Funnels** with drop-off and time-to-convert
- **Session replay** to watch what users did
- **Feature flags and A/B tests**
- **Surveys**
- **SQL access** for custom analysis

### Pricing

According to [PostHog's pricing page](https://posthog.com/pricing), the free tier includes **1 million product analytics events** and **5,000 session recordings** each month. Usage above that is billed on a pay-as-you-go basis, and allowances reset monthly.

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:** Large free tier, many tools in one, good for developers.

**Cons:** More technical than marketing-focused tools, usage costs need monitoring, interface can feel crowded.

## 6. Hotjar (Now Part of Contentsquare)

**Best for: Understanding why people drop off, using heatmaps, recordings and surveys.**

Hotjar is best known for heatmaps and session recordings, and its Observe product also includes funnels. It's ideal for pairing with a numbers-focused tool: once you know which step leaks, Hotjar helps you see what's going wrong on that page.

### Key Features

- **Heatmaps** showing clicks, moves and scrolls
- **Session recordings**
- **Funnels** inside the Observe product
- **On-site surveys and feedback widgets**

### Pricing

According to Usercall (updated October 2026), Hotjar pricing is moving to Contentsquare. Legacy Observe plans include a free **Basic** plan, **Plus at about $32 a month**, **Business at about $99 a month** and **Scale at about $213 a month** (billed annually). Contentsquare's new **Growth** plan is about **$49 a month**. Sources show different figures, so confirm current prices directly.

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:** Excellent for finding the "why," easy to set up, surveys included.

**Cons:** Pricing in transition, not a full funnel analytics replacement, free plan is limited.

## 7. Microsoft Clarity

**Best for: Free heatmaps and session recordings for any budget.**

Microsoft Clarity is a completely free behavior analytics tool. It records sessions, generates heatmaps and flags frustration signals like "rage clicks" and "dead clicks." It also integrates with GA4, so you can jump from a GA4 segment to the relevant recordings.

### Key Features

- **Free heatmaps and session recordings**
- **Rage click and dead click detection**
- **Funnels and filters** to focus on specific journeys
- **GA4 integration**

### Pricing

**Free.**

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:** Free, easy to install, great for spotting usability problems.

**Cons:** Not a full analytics platform, fewer advanced features than paid tools.

## Which Funnel Analytics Tool Should You Choose?

**Choose based on your business type, budget and whether you need to see where people drop off, why they drop off, or both.**

### For Bloggers and Small Businesses

**Start with GA4 and Microsoft Clarity, both free.** Together they show where people leave and why. If you're focused on local customers, this guide to [boosting a local business online](https://sites.google.com/view/boost-local-business-guide) shares ways to bring more visitors into your funnel.

### For Ecommerce Stores

**Use GA4 for ecommerce tracking plus Clarity or Hotjar for checkout behavior.** Add Funnelytics if you run multi-step funnels with upsells across several platforms.

### For Course Creators, Coaches and Agencies

**Funnelytics is often the best fit.** Its visual maps make webinar, launch and lead funnels easy to track and explain to clients.

### For SaaS and App Companies

**Choose Mixpanel, Amplitude or PostHog.** They track in-app behavior, onboarding and retention far better than marketing tools.

### For Developers and Startups on a Budget

**PostHog** offers a large free tier and many tools in one place.

## Free vs Paid Funnel Analytics Tools

**Free tools are enough for many small businesses, while paid tools save time and add depth as funnels become more complex.**

- **Free options:** GA4, Microsoft Clarity, Mixpanel Free, Amplitude Free, PostHog free tier, Hotjar Basic
- **When free is enough:** Simple funnels, low traffic and a small team
- **When to pay:** Complex funnels across domains, many campaigns, client reporting or large event volumes

A common path is to start free, then upgrade only when a specific limit, like data history, event volume or reporting time, starts to slow you down.

## How to Get the Most From Any Funnel Analytics Tool

**Define clear funnel steps, tag campaigns consistently, test your tracking and review data regularly.**

- **Map your funnel first**, so you know which steps to track
- **Name events consistently**, like `signup_completed` and `purchase_completed`
- **Use consistent UTM tags** across every campaign
- **Set up cross-domain tracking** for checkouts on other domains
- **Test the full funnel yourself** before trusting the data
- **Combine numbers and behavior**: use one tool for drop-off rates and another for heatmaps
- **Review weekly** for active campaigns and monthly for evergreen funnels

If you're comparing a visual funnel tool with general analytics, this [Funnelytics pricing and features review](https://bloggervoice.com/funnelytics-review/) explains where it fits alongside tools like GA4.

## Common Mistakes When Choosing a Tool

**Avoid paying for features you don't need, choosing a tool your team can't use and relying on one tool for everything.**

- **Buying an expensive tool too early**, before you have enough traffic
- **Choosing a product analytics tool for a marketing site**, or the other way around
- **Ignoring setup time** and the skills your team has
- **Not checking free plan limits**, like event caps or data history
- **Relying only on numbers** without behavior data to explain them
- **Not planning events** before installing a product analytics tool

## Final Thoughts

The best funnel analytics tool in 2026 depends on what you're tracking. GA4 is the free foundation for almost every website. Funnelytics is the strongest choice for visual marketing funnels, especially for agencies, coaches and course creators. Mixpanel, Amplitude and PostHog lead for SaaS and app funnels. And Hotjar and Microsoft Clarity show why people drop off.

For most businesses, the smartest setup is a combination: one tool to show where your funnel leaks and another to show why. Start with free tools, map your funnel clearly, and upgrade only when your funnels become complex enough to justify the cost.
